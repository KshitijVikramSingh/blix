# Tonemap lab

Offline experiments on tone mapping for Blix: reproduce Polyphony's GT7 operator as a baseline, then
fit and tune it for SDR and HDR. Nothing here ships in the engine; results come back as generated
constants or cooked data (see the handoff notes below).

```
cd lab/tonemap
uv sync            # Python 3.12, numpy, pytest (pytorch/slangpy are added when a lane needs them)
uv run pytest -q
```

## Making captures

```
uv run python scenes/make_scenes.py                       # -> data/scenes/*.glb (gitignored)
cd ../..
BLIX_SHOT_HDR=1 ./blix shot --model lab/tonemap/data/scenes/materials_grid.glb --out lab/tonemap/data/materials_grid.png
cd lab/tonemap && uv run python -m tmlab.report           # stats + out/5_capture_compare.png
```

`BLIX_SHOT_HDR=1` is a small hook in `Blix.Tools.Shot` (with `Blix.Graphics.Images.PfmWriter`): beside the PNG it
writes the scene target's radiance, before exposure and tonemap, as a PFM. Unset, the tool behaves as before.
Studio draws its debug grid, sun arrow and bounds into that target, so `shot` has a `--no-gizmos` flag; the lab
always passes it. `scenes/capture_set.py` captures every scene x lighting variant (dim, bright, dusk, grazing)
with Studio's own look flags (`--sun-intensity`, `--horizon-brightness`, ...) and records them in
`data/captures.json`. Sponza is deliberately not used: it is minutes of boot for one frame.

Statistics to know before fitting: in the lit scenes most pixels are ground and sky (median 0.05 to 0.6) and
the spheres carry the tail (max 2.5 to 4.2), so per-pixel averages are dominated by the stage; the emissive
ramp is the one with real range (max about 48, 8 to 10 stops from p1 to p99.9). Weight accordingly.

## Conventions

Linear light, frame-buffer units where 1.0 = 100 cd/m^2 (GT7's), colour channel last, float64.
GT7 works in linear Rec.2020; Blix's scene target primaries are not yet confirmed, so a
Rec.709 -> Rec.2020 step (`color.rec709_to_rec2020`) is the likely front end.

## Provenance

The GT7 algorithm is from Polyphony Digital's SIGGRAPH 2025 talk "Driving Toward Reality: Physically
Based Tone Mapping" (the slides' sample implementation is MIT-licensed, (c) 2025 Polyphony Digital
Inc.). This port follows its structure and is held to its numbers by `tests/golden`; the reference code
itself is not vendored. If any of this code is later promoted into `src/`, add a
`THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` entry.

## SDR fit (first result)

`uv run python -m tmlab.fit` (about 10 s) and `--pareto`; figures with `uv run python -m tmlab.fit_plots`. The target:
keep ACES's highlight roll-off, GT7's low hue shift and its colour, leave the response below scene 1.0 where GT7
puts it. Four terms in units of their own tolerance (roll-off 0.03 sRGB, hue 3 deg, chroma 0.03, toe 0.02) plus
validity penalties; fitted on synthetic sweeps only, scored on the eight captures, which it never sees.

The trade is between hue and colour, so the chroma weight is the knob (objective: baseline GT7 total 9.97):

| chroma weight | roll-off | hue | chroma | lit scenes keep chroma | emissive blown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| GT7 baseline | 0.42 | 8.94 | 0.61 | 93% | 79% |
| 1 | 0.083 | 0.24 | 1.33 | 74% | 57% |
| **4** | 0.077 | 0.89 | 0.92 | 84% | 40% |
| 16 | 0.170 | 4.37 | 0.43 | 89% | 38% |

Candidate (chroma weight 4): `alpha 0.001, gray_point 0.601, linear_section 0.286, toe_strength 1.237,
blend_ratio 0.869, fade_start 0.814, fade_end 1.209`. Read it with these in mind:

- `alpha` sits on its lower bound in every fit, so the bound is the constraint, not the data. Below zero the
  shoulder's asymptote falls under the display peak (white is never reached); that is a design decision.
- Part of the hue gain is colours whitening earlier (`fade_start` 0.98 -> 0.81), and the objective weights hue
  by the chroma a colour has left. That is why the chroma term exists, and why its weight is a judgement.
- Emissive highlights still blow out more than ACES (40% against 28%): this curve family has no ACES-like tail.
- Baseline GT7 SDR is not hue-stable near its peak (red +55, cyan -35, green -22 degrees in the last stop or two
  before it whitens out); that holds at the 250-nit anchor. HDR at 1000 nits was far steadier.
- SDR only. HDR has no ACES to match; its target is a separate decision.

## ACES reference, shoulder tail, HDR fit, HDR stills

- **The reference.** The `aces` in Blix is the Narkowicz 2015 fit, not ACES 2.x. `--reference aces2` measures against
  the real output transform, via OCIO 2.6 (`cg-config-v5.0.0_aces-v2.1_ocio-v2.6`). That config's views are still
  named "ACES 2.0 ..." and use the `..._2.0` builtins: OCIO has no separate 2.1 transform, so quote it as that.
  ACES maps scene 0.18 to 10 nits; its input is scaled until it agrees with baseline GT7 on mid grey (exposure
  1.707), so the comparison is of shape. ACES 2.x is far gentler than Narkowicz: 0.60 at scene 1.0, 0.97 at 16.
- **Shoulder tail** (`GT7Params.shoulder_tail`, ours, 0 = the published exponential exactly): needed to follow that
  tail. SDR roll-off error against ACES 2.x, objective total: baseline GT7 13.89, seven parameters 3.27, with the tail
  1.04. The tail and `alpha` trade off along a ridge (tail 3 -> 10 improved the total only 1.04 -> 0.97 while `alpha`
  moved 0.16 -> 0.58), so its bound is 3 and a solution at the bound is a ridge point, not a finding.
- **HDR fit** (`uv run python -m tmlab.fit_hdr`; peaks 400, 1000, 1600, 4000 fitted jointly). No reference operator, so
  the target is GT7's own premise: same grey below the knee, a shoulder with no clamp corner, stable hue, colour no
  lower than baseline, and a highlight floor (grey at 1, 2, 4, 8 x peak within `floor` of baseline). Without the floor
  the smooth shoulder dimmed a scene value at the peak to 57% of it (baseline 83%). Results at 1000 nits, emissive:

  | variant | clamp corner | hue | colour kept | grey at scene = peak |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | baseline GT7 | 3.30 | 6.04 | 61.9% | 83.5% |
  | seven parameters | 2.41 | 0.13 | 56.6% | 83.5% |
  | tail, floor 0.05 | 0.000 | 0.15 | 56.4% | 89.8% (odd parameters, distrust) |
  | **tail, floor 0.15** | 0.003 | 0.11 | 61.6% | 70.8% |
  | tail, floor 0.30 | 0.002 | 0.15 | 57.5% | 59.3% |

  Floor 0.15: `alpha 0.085, gray_point 0.548, linear_section 0.390, toe_strength 1.277, blend_ratio 0.965,
  fade_start 0.876, fade_end 1.194, shoulder_tail 1.013`. It is also slightly nearer ACES 2.x HDR than baseline GT7 on
  the emissive captures (Delta E ITP 6.9 against 7.4); on lit scenes it is level (4.2 against 3.9).
- **HDR stills** (`uv run python -m tmlab.hdr_export`): 16-bit PQ/Rec.2020 PNGs with a cICP chunk in `out/hdr_stills/`
  (open `index.html` or the files in Safari/Preview), baseline GT7, tuned, and ACES 2.x at 1000 and 1600 nits, plus an
  SDR control. macOS's `sips` reads them as "Rec. ITU-R BT.2100 PQ". Only the emissive scene reaches a shoulder; the
  material grid peaks near 780 nits at the 250-nit anchor, so its 1000 and 1600-nit stills are identical. How a viewer
  maps the absolute nits onto a panel depends on its headroom; the engine's own window cannot show HDR (SDR swapchain).
  Checked on the author's Liquid Retina XDR display: the PNG and the HEIC both read correctly in the macOS default app;
  a viewer that ignores cICP shows the PNG flat and washed out (PQ values read as sRGB), so prefer HEIC/AVIF to share.

## Chosen HDR parameters: floor 0.30

`results/hdr_tuned.json` (a committed result; `out/` is not): the published seven parameters plus `shoulder_tail`, one set
for every display peak, chosen by eye on a 1600-nit XDR display from sheets at floors 0.15, 0.20, 0.30 and 0.40.

    alpha 0.156  gray_point 0.540  linear_section 0.250  toe_strength 1.280
    blend_ratio 0.973  fade_start 0.875  fade_end 1.259  shoulder_tail 2.260

At 1000 nits (emissive captures, baseline GT7 -> tuned): no clamp corner at any peak; hue shift 2.85 -> 0.35 degrees;
colour kept 61.9% -> 65.7%; pixels pinned at the peak 5.0% -> 0.0%; a scene value exactly at the peak lands at 58% of the
peak instead of 83.5% (the cost: the brightest highlights are dimmer; at 2x the peak 72% instead of 100%); Delta E ITP
against ACES 2.x HDR 7.44 -> 7.67 (it moves slightly away from ACES, by design). `tests/test_results.py` holds these
properties. Fitted with 24 starts; an 8-start run at this floor was a local minimum. The earlier HDR stills in
`out/hdr_stills` were exported with the floor-0.15 parameters.

## Pushing the HDR floor, the night scene, comparison sheets

- **Floor sweep** (24 starts where it mattered; an 8-start 0.30 run landed in a local minimum and lost colour, so use many
  starts). Grey at scene = peak (baseline 83.5%) / colour kept on the emissive captures (baseline 61.9%) / Delta E ITP
  against ACES 2.x HDR (baseline 7.44): floor 0.15: 70.8% / 61.6% / 6.90; 0.20: 66.7% / 62.4% / 7.16;
  0.30: 58.4% / 65.7% / 7.67; 0.40: 56.0% / 64.8% / 7.63. Raising the floor buys colour and a smoother shoulder and
  moves away from ACES 2.x. Floors 0.15 and 0.20 sit in a different corner (tail 1.0, knee 0.39 against tail 0.1, knee 0.14).
- **`night_lamps`** (a dark scene, a dim window and screen, lamps from 4 to 4000 x emissive, neon, glossy spheres): median
  scene luminance 0.006, 22.6 stops from p1 to p99.9, which the stage scenes never reach. It is the one that shows what
  HDR is for. The comparison sheet's "things to look for" are written from what the sheet actually shows: on the grey
  stage tuned and baseline match, on saturated mid-tones they differ in colour (tuned blends 96% hue-preserving against
  60%), and the sheet labels each largest difference as brightness or colour.

## Auto-exposure, first attempt (a negative result, kept; superseded by the section below)

Target: a stated proxy (entropy of the output minus penalties for blown and crushed pixels, under baseline GT7 SDR),
not human labels; `--labels` overrides it per frame. Data: 45 frames (3 scenes x lightings x zooms), 9.7 stops of
target spread. Leave-one-scene-out RMS error in stops: textbook geometric mean with key 0.18 **1.45**, with a fitted key
1.52, 10-90% clipped log-average 1.68, median 2.04, arithmetic mean 2.54, our seven-parameter windowed model **2.87**
(in-sample 1.08). It fails on the held-out night scene (5.5 stops) because it never saw that range. Adding only a
two-parameter highlight protection to the geometric mean did not help either (best pooled 1.48 against 1.52).
The geometric mean's real weakness is systematic: it exposes emitter-heavy frames about 1.9 stops too bright, and no
luminance average can see that. Conclusion: keep the geometric mean (key 0.18) until there are more varied scenes or
human labels to learn a highlight-protection rule from. Temporal adaptation is not fitted (no data).

## Auto-exposure as adaptation dynamics

Why: GT7's slides (176-221) reject the camera model where every frame is properly exposed ("the night stays dark, a
snowy field stays blinding") and get those cases from the eye's limits instead: a short-term stage (pupil and neural gain,
3-5 EV, a few seconds) held inside a window set by a long-term stage (receptor sensitivity, 12 EV, tens of minutes).
`adaptation.py` models that in log2 luminance: S follows the metered scene level (faster brightening than darkening) and is
clamped to [A - range_dark, A + range_light], a window of 3 to 5 EV IN TOTAL; A follows slowly and is itself clamped to a
12 EV span, [light_limit - 12, light_limit]; exposure is `key - S`.

**Two interpretations, the lab's and not GT7's** (the slides give diagrams, 197-209 and 213-219, and no formulas):
the short-term "range 3-5 EV" is the total width of S's window (a first version, pushed in `0c18c446`, used +-4 EV, an
8 EV window, twice what the slides support); and the long-term "range 12 EV" caps how far A itself can move.

**What is and is not fitted.** GT7 publishes ranges and orders of magnitude, not constants, and there is no measured data
here, so nothing is fitted to data. The constants are TUNED to 18 written specs (the lab's own statements of the
behaviour GT7 describes) over nine scenarios: tunnel, cloud, a brighter day, snow, dusk, dawn, a 20 EV drop,
retroreflective signs and speculars, camera cuts. The defaults and the specs were written from the same description, so
the defaults already meet all 18 and tuning moved nothing; the table is a check on the structure, not on the constants.

- A plain single smoother misses 11 of 18 (it never stays dark in a tunnel, at dusk or after a 20 EV drop, never stays
  blinding on snow, is symmetric at dawn); the two-stage model with the 12 EV cap meets all 18.
- What the 12 EV cap does: over an hour the night settles 2 EV below proper and STAYS there (A cannot follow past
  its span); the snowy field stays 2 EV too bright indefinitely; a 20 EV drop stays 8 EV dark. A day only 3 EV brighter
  adapts within five minutes. Without the cap none of these persist.
- Where the specs pin each parameter (holding the others fixed; mostly free, so mostly taste): `light_limit` 0.6 to 3 EV
  above the day level (tight: bright days must adapt, the night must stay dark but visible), range_dark 1.85 to 2.9,
  tau_s_light 0.3 to 1.5 s, tau_a_dark at most about 690 s (dusk may lag by no more than 3 EV), tau_a_light 20 to 430 s.
  `trim_hi` and `w_world` interact (a higher trim helps speculars and hurts camera cuts, and the world weight pays for
  it): the joint region is trim 0.04 to 0.15 with w_world 0.4 to 0.87, and (0.09, 0.64) is its centre.
- **Retroreflective signs and speculars:** a 0.5% sign at +12 EV moves the metered level 0.02 EV and the exposure 0.01 EV;
  glints (1.5% at +9 EV) move the meter 0.05 EV; a sky filling 20% of the frame still moves it 1.0 EV, so the
  rejection is not blind.
- **Open and unverified: the tunnel.** With a 2 EV window per side an 8 EV tunnel stays about 6 EV dark for its whole
  length (`out/12_tunnel_filmstrip.png`: nearly black), where the first version was 4 EV dark. GT7's tunnels probably get
  help from the other references (sky visibility, light probes), which this model does not have. Real reference data
  would settle it.
- `light_limit` is an EV offset from the scenarios' day level. In an engine it would need an absolute luminance (the
  scene-white anchor gives nits), and what physical luminance the light limit sits at is unknown here.

Figures: `out/11_adaptation.png` (traces, the 12 EV span drawn), `out/12_tunnel_filmstrip.png`,
`out/13_dusk_filmstrip.png` (the same frame through GT7 SDR at each model's exposure).

Not modelled: white balance (GT7 adapts it by the same mechanism, on colour temperature and tint), real time-constant
data, and the position-independent references beyond a stand-in (`w_world`, exercised only by the camera-cut spec).
Glare and motion blur are after those.

## Colour-correction fit

`uv run python -m tmlab.grade [--tonemap baseline|tuned]`, figure with `tmlab.grade_plot`. Five parameters in HDR linear before the tone map: ev,
red and blue white-balance gains, a luminance-based contrast around 0.18 (hue-neutral; Blix's existing `blix_contrast`
pivots at 0.5 on raw values, which is not meaningful for HDR), and saturation in `blix_saturate`'s form. Target: ACES 2.x
SDR; scored by Oklab distance on luminance-stratified pixels (finer strata at the top, so the bright tail is not drowned by
the stage), held out by scene. Mean distance to the ACES look, ungraded -> graded:

| held out | GT7 baseline | GT7 tuned (SDR fit, chroma weight 4) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| emissive_ramp | 0.105 -> 0.023 | 0.091 -> 0.020 |
| materials_grid | 0.105 -> 0.020 | 0.099 -> 0.019 |
| night_lamps | 0.087 -> 0.028 | 0.084 -> 0.027 |

The folds agree on the parameters (baseline: ev -0.9, contrast 0.89 to 0.92, saturation 0.77 to 0.97, white balance about
0; tuned needs less: contrast 0.94, saturation 0.89). A global grade cannot do everything: on the night scene the dark
ground keeps a uniform residual (ACES's toe is darker than GT7's) and bright warm lights stay tinted where ACES
neutralises them (a highlight-dependent desaturation would be the next parameter). A target from artists would use the
same `fit_grade`. Not done: a LUT grade (the engine has no LUT path) and any change to `tonemap.glsl`.

## The interactive demo and HDR output

`src/Demos/Blix.Demos.TonemapLab` (see `docs/demos.md`) puts the lab's tone mapping in a window with sliders:
`BLIX_HDR=1 ./blix run Blix.Demos.TonemapLab`. Its GT7 shader is a third copy of the arithmetic (after the published C++
and `gt7.py`), so `--selftest out.pfm --peak P --gt7 tuned|published` renders the hue ramp through it and
`tests/test_glsl_parity.py` compares that with `gt7.py` per pixel: they agree to one half-float step (about 0.16% relative;
the target is RGBA16F). The renders come from `data/selftest`, or from the demo itself with `BLIX_PARITY=1`.

**Auto-exposure in the demo.** A meter pass renders the same world at 1/8 resolution, read back about ten times a second
(a small, rare device wait); `Adaptation.cs` is a C# port of `adaptation.py` (the two-stage model with the 12 EV span) and of
the trimmed histogram meter, and steps every frame. Every constant is a slider, a time scale lets a 30-minute dusk run in
half a minute (`Run dusk` at x60), and "Look at the tunnel" is an instant camera cut. The port is held to the python by
`tests/test_csharp_parity.py` (no window needed: `--adaptation-selftest`, `--meter-selftest`): the adaptation steps agree
to 1e-7 and the meter to 1e-15. `--trace --cut-at S --frames N` prints the adaptation per reading for an unattended run: a
cut into the tunnel moves the metered level 8.07 EV, and S falls to its window floor (the long-term level minus 2 EV) in about
a second and stays 6 EV dark, as the specs say. (An earlier tunnel was a small dark rectangle, and cutting to it moved the
meter by only 0.57 EV, because the bright world around it filled 93% of the frame; the interior is now a sector wide enough
that looking into it fills the screen.)

**HDR output on the window** is an engine change in `Blix.Graphics.Vulkan`: `BLIX_HDR=1` enables
`VK_EXT_swapchain_colorspace` on the instance and prefers RGBA16F in extended-linear sRGB; `IGraphicsDevice.HdrOutput` says
whether it got it. On this machine's MoltenVK the surface offers extended-linear sRGB, BT.2020 linear, HLG and HDR10, in
RGBA16F and 10-bit. An HDR surface takes linear light with no hardware encode (1.0 = UI white), so the mode is opt-in: every
present shader written for the sRGB surface would come out wrong on it. What the code can show is the values sent to the
surface (`--capture`: EDR patches 0.25 to 6.0 arrive exactly).

**Measured on the author's Liquid Retina XDR (2026-10-25): it works.** The window's layer is `CAMetalLayer` with
`wantsExtendedDynamicRangeContent` true (MoltenVK sets it; `MacEdr` only reports it), pixel format RGBA16Float, colour space
"sRGB IEC61966-2.1 Linear; extended range". `NSScreen`'s EDR headroom reads 3.998 while the window is up (potential 16.0),
and the EDR patches agree by eye: patches at 4.0 and 6.0 times UI white look the same, because everything at or above the
headroom clips. Headroom moves with the brightness setting and only rises once the window is displayed (an early probe read
1.0), so the demo reads it live (`IRenderHost.DisplayHeadroom`) and by default sets GT7's peak to UI white times headroom,
so the shoulder ends where the display does. The absolute UI white in nits is not known (a 1600-nit peak over a headroom of
4 would make it about 400); it does not change the relative behaviour, only how literally "nits" can be read.

## Real HDRIs

Ten Poly Haven 2k panoramas (CC0; `sources/hdris.json` records URL, md5, licence and the catalogue's own fields) cover
midday, sunrise, sunset, night, a mixed-light night and an artificial-light interior. `tools/fetch_hdris.py` fetches them into
`data/hdri` (gitignored: the bytes are a fetch, not part of the repo); `python -m tmlab.hdri` prints per-image statistics
(median, 99.9th percentile, range in stops, trimmed meter, colour cast). The demo takes one with `--hdri name`, or from the
Environment combo; the panorama is scaled so its trimmed meter sits where the noon world's does, then every adaptation and
tone-map control works on it as on the procedural world. Two things to know:

- The catalogue's `whitebalance` is the balance the photographer *processed* with, not a measured scene illuminant: 5300-5600 K
  for nearly every natural scene, sunsets included. It is no ground truth for auto white balance.
- Equirect statistics are weighted by solid angle (cos of latitude), and the engine's cubemap path stores Half, so the demo
  scales and clamps (60000) before the upload; a few suns would otherwise overflow to infinity.

The C# loader and meter are held to `hdri.py` by `tests/test_hdri_parity.py` (decoders agree to 1e-6 relative on pixels, the
meter to 1e-5 EV; ten of ten agreed to 2e-7 and 4.9e-8 when checked by hand). `out/14_hdri_views.png` is the contact sheet of
all ten through GT7 published at a fixed exposure.

## The display probe

`python -m tmlab.display` runs `tools/edr_probe.swift` and reads each screen's EDR headroom (current and potential). The peak
in nits is not reported by the OS, so the UI white is an inference, `peak / headroom`: on the author's panel, potential 16.0
over a 1600-nit spec gives 100 nits at lowest brightness (which matches macOS), and a current 3.998 gives about 400 nits now.
That is labelled as an inference in the code; a photometer would settle it.

## Literature constants

What the adaptation constants rest on, as of 2026-10-25. Nothing here is fitted to data; GT7 publishes ranges only.

| constant | value | status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `tau_a_dark` (long-term, darkening) | 600 s | supported: the slow phase of human dark adaptation is of this order (Webvision) |
| `tau_a_light` (long-term, brightening) | 90 s | chosen; no source found |
| `tau_s_light`, `tau_s_dark` | 0.4 s, 1.2 s | chosen to meet the lab's specs; no source found |
| short-term window | 2 EV below and above | GT7 slides (3-5 EV in total) |
| long-term span | 12 EV | GT7 slides |
| `light_limit` | 2 EV | chosen, pinned to 0.6-3 EV by the feasible-range analysis |

The pupil time constants and the Watson and Yellott pupil-diameter formula could not be obtained (the NASA PDF timed out, the
journal copy returned 403), so they are not used. The honest summary is that one constant has a source and the rest are
specified behaviour with constants chosen to meet it.

## Auto white balance

`tmlab/awb.py` (model, constants and the reasoning in its docstring), `tests/test_awb.py` (18 specs written before the constants
were set), and a C# port, `AutoWhiteBalance.cs`, held to it by `tests/test_awb_parity.py`. GT7's slide 222 gives the mechanism
(exposure's dynamics on colour temperature and tint, toward D65, with a faint virtual light and camera-AWB limits) and no
constants, so everything is chosen to meet the specs. Decisions (the author's, 2026-10-25): PARTIAL adaptation (strength 0.7:
a sunset stays warm, tungsten stays a little warm), a strength-0 opt-out for stylised scenes, and measurement from whatever the
renderer can supply: the demo's meter pass writes a sky mask to alpha, and sky pixels count for 0.15.

What the work found, because it is not obvious:

- Measured plainly (trimmed, saturation-discounted mean RGB of the frame) the ten HDRIs read 6100-11800 K: a blue sky and green
  grass fill the frame, so the frame mean says "cool, green light" for scenes that are not. That is the classic gray-world
  failure, and it is why the sky mask exists. Masked, the same scenes read close to neutral and the correction is small
  (2-3% in the gains on `out/15_awb_views.png`; venice_sunset keeps its warmth).
- The tint limit is tight (0.06) on purpose: natural light barely varies in tint and a frame's green is mostly foliage.
- A strong cast is read slightly low (the saturation discount discounts everything once a cast saturates every pixel): the
  safe side of the error, with partial adaptation on top.
- The HDRI catalogue's own `whitebalance` is no test of this (see above), so the real-data check is the contact sheet, not a number.

The demo: `--awb off` starts it disabled; "auto white balance" has the sliders; the manual K / tint sliders apply on top.

## Glare and motion blur

**Glare** (`tmlab/glare.py`, `results/glare_fit.json`, `tests/test_glare.py`). The diffraction PSF of a circular aperture is the Airy
pattern, whose whole shape lives in r / a (a = 1.22 lambda N / pixel pitch, the first dark ring). So one fit serves every F-number
and only the scale changes; GT7's slides describe 24 hand-calibrated parameters per F-number for ten F-numbers, which this
scaling law makes unnecessary for the diffraction part (it says nothing about scatter in a real lens, which this does not model).
The ring-averaged envelope falls as r^-3 over decades; a Gaussian sum follows it, fitted in log space over 0.05 to 400 Airy radii:

| terms | max log10 error | factor | encircled-energy error |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | 1.02 | x10.6 | 0.055 |
| 4 | 0.57 | x3.7 | 0.014 |
| 5 | 0.33 | x2.1 | 0.007 |
| **6 (used)** | **0.19** | **x1.56** | **0.003** |
| 8 | 0.10 | x1.25 | 0.008 |

Six is the demo's cost/accuracy choice (eight is better but adds a term of passes). In the demo each term is the scene blurred
at the mip its width needs (`Glare.cs`, one `PostChain` per term; at most a 33-tap separable blur), composited in `present.frag`
in scene-linear light before exposure: mix 0 is off, 1 the full PSF; the Airy radius in pixels is the scale slider. The fit
exists in three files and `tests/test_glare_constants.py` holds them equal.

**Motion blur** (`tmlab/motionblur.py`, `tests/test_motionblur.py`). The mean of the world over the shutter, in scene-linear
light, done per pixel in `scene.frag` (the camera only rotates, so each sample is the same world at a nearby view angle). The
specs: the smear conserves the lamp's light, and averaging radiance keeps a 5000x lamp's streak bright where averaging
tone-mapped values would not. `--pan deg/s` and `--shutter` drive it unattended; `out/16_glare_blur.png` is the sheet.
The blur sweep is the frame-to-frame turn times the shutter, so it is small at 60 Hz (a 90 deg/s pan at a 180 degree shutter
sweeps 0.75 degrees): it needs a fast mouse flick or the pan slider to show.
