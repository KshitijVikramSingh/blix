Still the register. No longer the argument.

This page counts the tree, and counting is what it is for — so unlike its two companions from the same day it has been re-measured rather than retired. Every figure above and below now reads off 2aa1faa on main instead of 2d3f478 on the deleted character-lab branch.

What changed structurally, and why the family counts moved:

  • The toolchain lab became tools. Blix.Labs.Toolchain and its three executables are now Blix.Tools.Studio, .Shell, .View, .Shot and .Check — which is why labs fell from 8 to 4 and tools rose to 9. The rename was the point: Lab named nothing true.
  • An app layer landed. [BlixApp], a build-time index read from ECMA-335 metadata, and blix as a resolver — so the fourteen launcher scripts collapsed into one, and tools/ fell from 23 entries to 20.
  • Verification grew a shared tally. Blix.Verify, 129 lines and zero references, replaced six copies of the same counter — and Blix.Test.Studio split out of check, which turned out to be two tools sharing a name.
  • The two glTF importers became one extraction path. GltfStaticImporter 859 → 616 and GltfImporter 653 → 446, with the shared 289 in GltfShared. §03's file lists below reflect it.

§01's framing is the part that did not survive. It reads the four overlap cases as extraction decisions — what has earned the right to move. That question produced correct local answers and the wrong global one. Duplication is a symptom; what actually shapes a session is how much apparatus you must build before you learn anything. The four cases are annotated in place below with what each looks like under the later lens.

01 — the line

Engine and consumer, and the four things that are genuinely arguable

The split is cleaner than it feels from inside. The engine is a library stack that never names a game; every consumer is an executable, a tool, or a lab library serving one. What is worth arguing about is a short list — though not, it turned out, for the reason given here.

Read this section as a census, not as a verdict. The four cases below are real and the counts are current. What was wrong was treating "does this have a second consumer?" as the question that decides the shape of the tree. Each card now carries a second line saying what it looks like under the lens that replaced it.

Blix/SkinnedGameObject.cs
121 ln · in the engine · 1 consumer

A skinned model you can draw, offered to everyone. VulkanLit took it. Runner, Bulwark, RTSGame and the studio each wrote their own instead — because it bakes in a draw, and the draw is the part that differs.

Still one consumer, and still correct to leave. Under the later lens the finding is not that five projects duplicated a draw — it is that writing one costs a render graph, six shaders and a camera first. Blix.Tools.Studio is the answer to that, and it deliberately does not absorb this.

Blix.Render/PropModel.cs
686 ln · in the engine · 1 consumer

The same story one layer up, and the largest file in Blix.Render. Only RTSGame uses it — now from three of its files. Two engine-side attempts at one abstraction, both stranded, both for the same reason.

Unchanged, and no longer framed as debt. A 686-line module serving one 72,720-line game well is a module that found its consumer, not one that failed to find four.

Blix.Tools.Studio/StudioRig.cs
458 ln · outside · resolved — 5 consumers

glTF import, GPU buffers, textures, bounds, weighted-bone analysis — then a bone material that names a shader. The first half is what the character lab would otherwise copy. The second half is what stranded the other two.

Resolved, and not by extraction. It did not move into the engine; the lab around it was renamed to what it always was. Five consumers now, one of them headless with no device at all — which is the property that made the rename honest rather than cosmetic.

Blix.Labs.Character/BodyResolver.cs
183 ln · outside · stays out

Sweep-and-slide. Its math already moved into Blix.Geometry; the loop did not. The camera was expected to be its second consumer and turned out to want one cast and a clamp — decided by evidence, not deferral.

The one card that was always asking the right question, because it tested a prediction against a real second consumer and let the answer be no.

The pattern in all four: geometry and loading travel; a draw does not. An abstraction that names a shader program, a material set or a pass has an owner whether it admits one or not. That observation held. What did not hold is the conclusion drawn from it — that the tree's problem was things failing to travel. Three of these four were correct exactly as they stood, and the fourth was fixed by renaming a folder rather than by moving any code.

04 — the register

Every consumer, and what it is for

Twenty-four projects in four families. Descriptions come from each project's own header block — most demos carry an explicit proves / intentionally owns pair, which is the convention that keeps a demo from quietly becoming a game.

Demos

11 executables · 10,430 ln

Roughly two kinds, and the tree does not label which is which. The first five are executable specs — deliberately minimal call sites that exist so a primitive has a known-good consumer. The last four are games that grew from one.

Blix.Demos.Chassis
exe149 ln4 deps

The smallest application that can exist, and an executable spec for the chassis itself. It answers two questions nothing else can, because every other app is also a diagnostics producer: does an app get a UI without being IDebuggable, and does UI capture actually suppress game input. Deliberately not IDebuggable.

tools/run-chassis.sh
Blix.Demos.VulkanHello
exe511 ln8 deps

A spinning lit cube — the narrowest known-good Vulkan path: depth attachment, descriptor sets, per-frame UBO, name-keyed ShaderUniform through a UniformBlockLayout. Kept minimal on purpose so it is the simplest reference call site when something further up the stack breaks.

tools/run-vulkan-hello.sh
Blix.Demos.VulkanInstanced
exe180 ln7 deps

The instancing proof gate: 5,000 cubes in one vkCmdDrawIndexed, each reading its transform and tint from a set-3 SSBO. Auto-exits after more frames than there are frames in flight, so both replicated SSBO slots are written and bound at least once — which is what would surface an in-flight hazard.

tools/run-instanced.sh · assert under BLIX_VK_VALIDATE=1
Blix.Demos.VulkanGraph
exe372 ln8 deps

Render-graph validation in three passes: a cube into an HDR offscreen, an invert pass reading it through a graph Read edge, then an imperative present. If the edge is wired the cube appears inverted; if it is broken the present shows garbage. The failure is visible rather than silent, which is the whole design.

Blix.Demos.VulkanParticles
exe647 ln8 deps

Three CPU-simulated effects — a spark fountain with drifting smoke, a periodic explosion, a swirling vortex — riding the per-frame transient vertex arena through an HDR bloom pipeline with soft-particle depth fade. ParticleBatch stays pure geometry: the demo brings the pipeline, the push and the depth binding.

tools/run-particles.sh
Blix.Demos.VulkanLit
exe1,856 ln8 deps

PBR end to end: cube, skinned glTF, PBR spheres and ground under a sun, two spots and a point light, each with PCF shadows, plus procedural-sky IBL, HDR bloom and ACES tonemap. Shadow passes → lit scene → bloom → present.

It is also the only consumer of Blix/SkinnedGameObject.

Blix.Demos.VulkanSponza
exe2,329 ln11 deps

The Khronos Intel Sponza scene — the renderer's performance and asset-pipeline proving ground, and the most dependency-heavy project in the tree. Cooked siblings only (.blixtex, .blixprobe, .blixmesh with LOD chains). Shadow cascades → optional froxel fog → depth pre-pass → 4× MSAA lit scene → tonemap.

The tree's only consumer of Matrix4x4ArrayUniform, and therefore the only thing that would exercise the record-time array freeze. Its asset pack lives on an external SSD, so it is currently unverified.
tools/run-vulkan-sponza.sh · needs BLIX_SPONZA_ASSETS
Blix.Demos.Pong
exe711 ln10 deps

The 2D game proving the Vulkan SpriteBatch + Font path with a CRT post-FX finish. Gameplay runs in a virtual playfield; sprites render to a 2× offscreen through a Vulkan-NDC ortho, then a CRT shader downsamples to the swapchain. Owns its rules, physics, scoring and look.

Known fault, attributed and unfixed: destroys pipeline, buffer, framebuffer and render pass while a command buffer still references them.
tools/run-pong.sh
Blix.Demos.Runner
exe849 ln10 deps

A 3D endless runner — the instancing plus skeletal-animation showcase. The world flows toward the camera and wraps procedurally; ground, barrels and coins each draw through a per-mesh InstancedBatch, and the player is a skinned glTF character on the bone-palette path whose run cadence tracks speed and switches to a jump clip mid-air.

Its CC0 KayKit Rogue is the tree's reference rig — 41 bones, 76 clips — and the subject of every toolchain and character lab capture.

tools/run-runner.sh
Blix.Demos.TankArena
exe1,335 ln9 deps

A survival shooter built on Transform3D parenting. Each tank is a hull → turret → barrel hierarchy: driving the hull carries the turret, which yaws independently. Shells spawn as a child of the firing barrel, then SetParent(null, keepWorldPose) detaches them into a physics arc — the parenting model's detach op, demonstrated rather than described.

Its enemy steering was the first consumer to pressure a navigation primitive.

tools/run-tank.sh
Blix.Demos.Bulwark
exe1,491 ln9 deps

Tower defence, game #2, built specifically to pressure axes Runner, Pong and TankArena left alone: pointer-driven picking, navigation, and a skinned animated crowd through skinned-mesh instancing. Sun shadow → HDR scene → present, with a SpriteBatch HUD on the present pass.

Its nav and turret rig were deliberately not extracted.

Tools & labs

2 libraries + 7 executables + 1 widget · 9,454 ln

The pattern both share: one library holds the scene, shaders and renderer; several thin executables ask different questions of it. A viewer you drive, a judge that exits non-zero, a capture that writes a PNG. None of the executables declare shaders or render code.

The first group is no longer a lab. Blix.Labs.Toolchain and its three executables became Blix.Tools.Studio, .View, .Check and .Shot — the rename was the point, because Lab named nothing true about a library eight projects were already standing on. The character lab below is still a lab, and is still expected to be deleted.

Blix.Tools.Studio
lib2,261 ln5 consumers

Blix's opinionated 3D tooling stage — StudioRenderer (635), StudioRig (458), RigSession (389), StudioModel (247), StudioViews (133), plus camera, graph and push layout. Authored with taste rather than extracted under pressure: nothing is required to use it, and the two games with hand-built graphs keep them.

A tool contributes draws through IStudioView rather than being one of a fixed set of subjects — the case that forced it is terrain, which is neither a model nor a rig. The look is declared as [Tune], so every tool on the stage gets a Scene panel and --sun-elevation without parsing one.

LabSkeletonView did not come with it — it became Blix/Gizmos/SkeletonGizmo.cs, in the engine, because drawing a skeleton is not a tooling opinion.
Blix.Tools.View
exe1,741 ln8 deps

Chassis conventions over a real lit scene, with ImGui panels for the rig, its clips, instancing and bone masks. Every descriptor set, UBO offset and push range comes from spirv-cross sidecars rather than a hand-written interface — reflected binding, proved by being driven.

The largest tool in the tree, and the one the next arc is aimed at: its own half is the rig, the clips and the trail; the other half is a camera, three aspects, two view declarations and a viewport round-trip that every future tool would write again.
blix run view -- --rig …/Rogue.glb --clip Walking_A --mask …
Blix.Tools.Check
exe640 ln2 deps

The binding model printed — and checked. Deliberately not merged with blix-cook inspect: that lists what is in a file and always exits 0; this judges clip lengths, the skeleton, the binding contracts and layer masks, and exits non-zero when they are wrong. They overlap in subject and not in purpose.

It was two tools sharing a name. Its binding-model half — which needed no asset and no device — split out as Blix.Test.Studio (194), and running the remainder bare on a bad file was what exposed that it crashed on one, exit 134, rather than refusing it. It now catches AssetImportException and nothing else.
Blix.Tools.Shot
exe944 ln7 deps

Renders the lab and writes what it rendered to a PNG — the capability Blix did not have at all until this arc, since colour attachments were created without TransferSrcBit. Reproducible single-frame sampling, --xray, --frames-out, --zoom, and --lockstep as a negative control: N bodies on one clip must come back as exactly one pose fingerprint.

blix run shot -- --rig … --out shot.png
Blix.Labs.Character
lib2,018 ln3 consumers

The character lab's library — RoomCamera (382, four rigs), CharacterMotor (365), Room (301), RoomRenderer (293), RoomGeometry (196), BodyResolver (183), plus LabReport and LabTrace. Subject: contact — where a body can go and what stops it.

It has no character in it. The body is a capsule; nothing here loads a rig.

Blix.Labs.Character.Room
exe633 ln8 deps

The viewer you drive. Its central claim is one source: what is drawn and what will be swept are the same triangles, and the flat shading means every facet you can see is a facet the sweep will hit. The slope tint shades each surface by the normal the collider reads, so a mis-wound face reads as a floor standing up rather than as odd lighting.

tools/run-room.sh
Blix.Labs.Character.Probe
exe830 ln2 deps

87 checks, no window and no device — the room's ground truth is closed form, so verifying it is arithmetic. Every number in the room was authored, so "it looks like a ramp" is not the question; whether the ramp the sweep hits is the 30° the claim says is. A resolver resting at 31° and one sliding at 29° look identical in a screenshot.

Blix.Labs.Character.Capture
exe343 ln9 deps

The room as a PNG, headless and reproducible. It caught the ramps facing a wall — twice — when every probe check passed, which is the clearest case in the arc for keeping both a judge and a picture.

Tests

6 executables + 1 shared tally · 6,045 ln

All six are the same shape: a CLI harness printing GREEN OK / RED FAIL per case and exiting non-zero if any fails. No test framework, no attributes — the exit code is the contract. Every claim is paired with a negative control, on the rule that a test that can only pass is not a test.

The shape is now written down once. Blix.Verify — 129 lines, sealed, zero project references, because a tally must not depend on what it counts — replaced six private copies of the same counter. The four small suites average 205 lines, which is the size at which adding a seventh is a cheap decision rather than an arc.

Blix.Test.Graphics
exe3,272 ln516 checks

The engine suite, CPU-only, organised into lettered sections A through AU that each pin one decision — matrix convention, barrier equality, the reflected binding model, the uniform arena, and most recently bone masks and masked blending. It began as the acceptance criteria for the row-vector matrix migration and never stopped growing.

Blix.Test.Diagnostics
exe1,603 ln206 checks

The debug system's frame lifecycle, channels, history and dump sinks. It is the suite that should have caught JsonDumpSink serialising every System.Numerics value as {} for the life of the feature, and did not — nothing asserted on a published vector's contents.

Blix.Test.Physics2D
exe238 ln43 checks

The 2D collision math. Each primitive pair gets a known overlap, a known disjoint case and — where it means anything — an edge case: touching exactly, concentric, AABB aligned with OBB. Raycasts get a hit and a miss.

Blix.Test.Physics3D
exe277 ln35 checks

The suite Blix.Geometry had never had, added by the character arc. The docs described Intersection2D as mirroring the 3D pattern and being pressure-tested by the 2D harness — so the mirror was tested and the original it mirrored was not, and had not been touched since the initial commit. It starts at the capsule because a capsule is what a character is.

05 — tools

tools/ — 20 scripts, three jobs, and one fewer reason to exist

Every headed application has a launcher, and they all exist for one reason: Homebrew's dotnet is a #!/bin/bash shim, /bin/bash is SIP-protected, and dyld strips DYLD_* from its environment — so the Vulkan loader path never survives dotnet run. Each script sets the ICD and layer paths and then execs the apphost directly.

Launchers · 12
exec the apphost, never dotnet run

run-chassis · run-vulkan-hello · run-instanced · run-particles · run-vulkan-sponza · run-pong · run-runner · run-tank · run-bulwark · run-rts-game · run-lab · run-lab-capture

Two are already gone. run-room and run-room-capture were deleted when the character lab moved onto the app layer — they are now blix run character:room. The prologue they all carried lives once, in tools/blix, which execs the resolver; the remaining twelve are what has not been migrated yet rather than what still needs to exist.

Each carries its application's argument documentation in its header, which is where the flags are actually documented — --rig, --clip, --mask, --frames, --xray, --lockstep.

Gates & measurement · 3
judge, or tabulate

gate-rts-game.sh — the RTS gate: three quick runs by default, --years adds the two simulated years. Only for RTS work; engine changes go through the four suites instead.

perf-matrix.sh — the camera and daylight matrix run as one command and tabulated from the runs themselves. perf-pacing.sh — the vsync-on pacing pass, with repeats.

Asset pipeline · 5
cook, merge, convert

cook-character.sh — turns a rigged character plus a folder of single-animation files into one .glb the game can load, then reports what bound. character_merge.py is the merge step: every clip on one skeleton, the shape GltfImporter requires.

setup-sponza-modern.sh populates the Sponza packs into a split raw/cooked layout; cook-sponza-modern.sh encodes every PNG and JPEG into side-by-side .blixtex. vdb_to_blix_volume.py converts VDB volumes.

Not a script · 1
imgui.ini

The saved ImGui layout, shared by every application that hosts panels. It is why a lab viewer opens with its windows where you left them, and why deleting it resets every app at once.

Also outside src/: docs/ (6 files, including conventions.md — the numbered §-index the commit messages cite), perf/ (154 files of captured measurement runs), third_party/ (27 files), and seven plan-*.md documents at the repo root, which are the arcs' written records rather than their summaries.

07 — pressure

What the graph says needs attention

Findings, not opinions — each is a number off the tree above, ordered by how much of the structure it implicates rather than by how easy it is to fix.

Re-checked rather than reprinted. Every one of these still holds at 2aa1faa, which is worth saying because two of them were expected to have moved. What has changed is what each is taken to mean — a count of five projects writing their own skinned draw is a fact about cost, not a backlog of extractions owed.

2.1×game vs engine

RTSGame is larger than everything it stands on

72,720 lines against the engine's 34,981, and 72% of all non-engine code. Whatever it has learned about geometry, nav, crowds and rendering has not travelled — and the empty Geometry cell says that is not an accident of style.

6skinned paths

Six independent ways to draw a skinned mesh

Runner, VulkanLit, the studio, Bulwark, RTSGame and SkinnedGameObject — each with its own vertex shader. The pose pipeline underneath is shared by all of them; only the draw is copied, because only the draw was ever baked into the abstraction. Unchanged, and now read differently: the studio's copy is the one a tool can take without writing it, which was the point of authoring it.

807stranded lines

Two engine abstractions with one consumer each

SkinnedGameObject (121, VulkanLit) and PropModel (686, RTSGame, now from three of its files). Both are shaped as shared infrastructure and neither is. Both were left exactly as they are, and that is the finding: a module serving one consumer well is not debt. What the third attempt did instead was not enter the engine at all — Blix.Tools.Studio sits outside it, and nothing is required to use it.

22,784lines of debug

A third of the game is instrumentation

RTSGame's Debug/ folder is larger than any engine module and four times its Rendering/. None of it is reusable by anything else, and the labs rebuilt their own equivalents — report, trace, probe — from scratch.

8,267lines, one module

Blix.Graphics.Vulkan is 24% of the engine

The largest module, split across ten VulkanGraphicsDevice.* partials plus the render graph. One dependency and 21 consumers, so the weight is contained — but swapchain and textures alone are 2,927 lines.

1unverified consumer

The array-uniform freeze has never been exercised

The prologue froze Matrix4x4ArrayUniform and built a record-time detector. VulkanSponza is the tree's only consumer of it and its asset pack is on an unmounted SSD, so the fix that started this whole arc is still unproven against the one thing that would prove it.

0emitters

The cooker has no consumers, and no load says which path it took

The Tools.Cook column is empty in §06 — nothing can call the cooker, because it is a command rather than something a tool can ask. And AssetLoadReport names the four states a load can be in (Source, Cooked, Fallback, Missing) with zero emitters: its only references are the test that checks it round-trips. 30 of the tree's 62 mesh assets are cooked and the line between them is which directory somebody typed. Counted in full in The Unfinished Cook.

2folders, no csproj

src/ contains things the build graph cannot see

Blix.Shaders is three GLSL includes reached by glslc -I; Blix.Labs.Character.Motion is a deleted project's leftover bin/ and obj/. Both make ls src lie about what the tree is — and both are still here, which is its own small finding about how long a leftover survives once nothing fails because of it.
